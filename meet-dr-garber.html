Why I built the bioformulas

Nobody had combined all three. So I did.

The turning point in my career didn’t happen in a clinic. It happened in a classroom.

By 1995, the demands of running Los Angeles’ first comprehensive integrative health clinic had begun to overshadow the work I loved most: helping patients heal. I took a sabbatical to return to the study and practice of homeopathy on a deeper level. It was there that I heard Dr. Trevor Cook, homeopath to the Queen Mother, speak about biotherapies. In that moment, I knew I had discovered something that would change the way I practiced medicine.

There were three biotherapies. One made from young plant parts. One from mineral-rich rocks. One from healthy organ tissue. Each had been used successfully for generations.

GemmotherapyMade from the buds and young shoots of plants, the parts still actively growing, used to support the body’s drainage and repair. LithotherapyMade from mineral-rich rocks, prepared homeopathically to supply the trace minerals cells rely on to regulate themselves. OrganotherapyMade from healthy organ tissue, prepared homeopathically to encourage a corresponding organ to return to normal function.

Yet, after more than two centuries of homeopathy, no one had ever combined all three in a single formula. No one had even asked what might happen if they were combined together.

I went back to my graduated cylinders and Erlenmeyer flasks. I started blending fig tree buds with silver birch seeds, then adding lepidolite, and finally hypothalamus extract, bringing together all three biotherapies in a single formula.

Then I gave them to patients whose chronic conditions had stopped responding to everything else.

They responded.

The experiment had become a new way of practicing medicine.

That was the beginning of the bioformulas.

Why my approach is different

Two things I learned that most practitioners will not say out loud.

I do not have to be the one who fixes you.

My goal has always been to help people get better. But not long after I started at Westside, I realized something that changed the way I practiced.

Even when a patient didn’t get better with me, I had still helped them. I had become part of their healing journey. Sometimes I was the practitioner who helped them recover. Sometimes it was the chiropractor, acupuncturist, nutritionist, herbalist, or physician they saw before me or after me.

What matters is not who gets the credit. What matters is the patient. You.

Healing is rarely the result of one practitioner. More often, it’s the result of many people contributing at the right time. If I move you one step closer to health, then I’ve done my job.

There is never only one way in.

On my first morning back from sabbatical, my very first patient came in with severe sciatica.

Before my sabbatical, I would have adjusted him and sent him home. Instead, I wanted to know whether homeopathy alone could help.

So I prescribed a remedy.

His sciatica disappeared.

That day, I realized something profound: neither I nor my patients were limited to a single modality. Chiropractic, homeopathy, acupuncture, herbal medicine, each offers a different path to healing.

What matters isn’t the modality. What matters is finding the approach that gives the body what it needs to heal.

Healing doesn’t belong to a single modality, because there is never only one way in.

People and animals

Within a year I had earned my Animal Chiropractic Certification, started working with Assistance Dogs of the West, joined the board of the New Mexico Center for Therapeutic Riding, and adopted more Springer Spaniels.

Then I found out my clients had quietly been giving my human formulas to their dogs and horses, and getting results. So I went back to the lab and did it properly. Canine first, then the equine line: horses, donkeys, ponies, mules.

Same biotherapies. Same standards. Chemistry built for the animal taking them.

The kid who assisted a vet on Long Island had come full circle.
